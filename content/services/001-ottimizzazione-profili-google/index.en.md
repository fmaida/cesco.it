## What you get right away

* **More bookings from your customers,** who will have more ways to get in touch with you: phone, email, social media and, if you like, a dedicated page of your own. And if you already use a booking service such as *TheFork* or *OpenTable*, I link it to your listing, so customers can book a table at any hour, wherever they are.

* **Fewer customers walking away angry and leaving a bad review.** What is one of your worst nightmares? I'll tell you one of mine, from when my parents owned a restaurant: customers turning up to find the place closed. Day off or holidays, it makes no difference. Today an angry customer standing in front of a closed shutter almost always becomes a customer who leaves you a bad review. And you'll be forced to explain to Google, famously slow on the uptake about these things, that this customer never actually ate at your place. By fixing your profile and keeping the information up to date, I reduce this risk.

* **Fewer complaints.** Because it's clear from the start what you offer and what you don't: information you can rely on in front of the customer and in the eyes of Google. If a customer complains that they "found it closed" or that "there was nothing for vegetarians", you have an up-to-date listing to point them to, and a stronger public reply to give them and Google.

* **Happy customers who walk into your place already knowing what they'll find.**

## Why do it?

Every day tens of thousands of tourists visit your city, relying on Google Maps and Google's other tools to find their way around. At any time of day or night, when your customer is looking for a restaurant, a hotel or a shop, Google can not only give them the addresses of the nearest ones, but also compare them by reputation and show photos of the shop and of the products or services on offer.

Your business probably already has a Google profile, created by you or perhaps by someone else without your knowledge. **But do you really know what it tells your potential customers about you?**

### It's understandable that you don't have time for everything. But have you ever thought about the consequences?

You've probably already noticed in the picture:

* The complete lack of an introduction explaining what makes the restaurant unique and which signature dishes should make it a place worth trying.

* The complete lack of a phone number to call for more information and perhaps to book a table.

* The complete lack of a menu: what will I eat if I walk into this restaurant? If I take my vegetarian girlfriend, will there be anything for her?

* The complete lack of an online booking system that lets people book a table without having to go to the restaurant in person.

* Nobody has claimed the listing. This means that any ill-intentioned competitor can happily do one or more of these nice little things:

  * Change the opening days and hours to make the restaurant look closed

  * Change the address to send people to a completely different part of the city, or to the competing restaurant

  * Change the phone number and/or add a link to the competitor's website, literally stealing the page's visitors and getting them to book at their place instead of at these unlucky owners'.
